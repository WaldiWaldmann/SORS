Wind power, designed for every place.

SORS designs small wind turbines for the exact spot where they will stand, so off-grid communities can build, run and repair them locally.

Without power, families burn kerosene inside their homes and pay for diesel they can't afford. Clinics can't keep vaccines cold. Children study by candlelight.

Solar has brought light, but panels stop producing at sundown. Small wind could fill the night, yet it rarely reaches these places. Imported turbines are built for textbook wind, not the gusty, low-speed wind of a real village, and they stop working when a spare part is missing.

The communities that did least to cause climate change face its harshest heat and droughts without the energy to adapt.

A rotor that adapts, and software that knows the site.

Each blade is split into segments. A small controller turns them to match the wind, so the rotor keeps working when the wind changes instead of only at one design speed.

    Schematic illustration of how segment control works. The angles shown are for explanation only, not measurement data.

    Software

    • Our own physics engine based on blade element momentum theory
    • Genetic optimization with adaptive segment weighting
    • SORS v4 turns local wind data into a site-specific design report

    Thirteen prototypes in, and the numbers hold up.

    1. 13 prototypes

      Built and tested with different blade materials and at different sites. Each one taught us something for the next.

    2. Within 3.2%

      After calibration, our simulation matches measured prototype performance within about 3.2%. That means we can trust the software to design new turbines.

    3. SORS v4

      Local wind data goes in, a complete site-specific design report comes out. No engineering background needed.

    4. First users

      The people of our home village are the first to use power from SORS prototypes.

    Starting in northern Kenya.

    In Marsabit and Turkana counties the wind is so strong that the Lake Turkana Wind Power plant feeds more than 17% of the power on Kenya's national grid. Yet in Turkana, 91% of households have no grid connection.

    Power when the sun is gone

    Wind often blows when solar is weak, so a health post can keep its lights on through the night.

    Lower costs, lower emissions

    Every litre of diesel and kerosene replaced saves money, cleans the air at home and cuts carbon emissions.

    Ownership, not dependence

    Printable parts and off-the-shelf electronics mean local technicians can build, install and repair every turbine.
